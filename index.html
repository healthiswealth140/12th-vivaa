Section 01 · Yoga

Introduction to Yoga What it is and why we practise it

What is Yoga?

Yoga is an ancient Indian practice that brings the body, mind and breath together. The word yoga comes from the Sanskrit word yuj, which means "to join" or "to unite". Regular practice makes us stronger, calmer and more focused.

The three main parts of yoga

  • Asana — physical postures such as Tadasana or Bhujangasana.
  • Pranayama — breathing exercises such as Anulom Vilom.
  • Dhyana — meditation and relaxation of the mind.

Why is yoga useful for a student?

  • Improves body posture and flexibility.
  • Reduces stress and helps concentrate during exams.
  • Builds strength without heavy equipment.
  • Helps prevent lifestyle diseases like obesity and diabetes.
?Quick Viva
What is yoga?
An ancient Indian practice that joins the body, mind and breath through postures and breathing exercises.
What is an asana?
A steady, comfortable physical posture held for some time.
What is pranayama?
Control of breathing — slow, deep and rhythmic breathing exercises.

Section 01 · Yoga

Tadasana The Mountain Pose

Helps withObesity · Asthma · Hypertension · Back Pain
TypeStanding · Beginner
Fig. 1 — Tadasana, final position

How to do it

  1. Stand straight with feet together and hands by your sides.
  2. Interlock your fingers and turn the palms upward.
  3. Breathe in and stretch both arms above your head.
  4. Slowly raise your heels and balance on your toes.
  5. Stretch your whole body upward and hold for 5–10 seconds.
  6. Breathe out, bring your heels down, and relax.
Benefits
  • Improves posture and body alignment.
  • Strengthens thighs, knees and ankles.
  • Improves balance and body awareness.
Precautions
  • Avoid if you have vertigo or low BP.
  • Do not hold the breath for too long.
  • Keep the spine straight, not bent.
?Quick Viva
Why is Tadasana called the Mountain Pose?
Because in the final position the body is tall, still and firm like a mountain.
Which muscles are mainly used?
Thighs, calves, ankles, back and shoulder muscles.

Section 01 · Yoga

Katichakrasana The Waist Twisting Pose

Helps withObesity · Diabetes · Hypertension · Back Pain
TypeStanding twist · Beginner
Fig. 2 — Katichakrasana, twisting to the right

How to do it

  1. Stand erect with your feet about 30 cm apart.
  2. Raise both arms to shoulder level, palms facing each other.
  3. Breathe in and swing both arms to the right.
  4. Twist your waist to the right. Keep the right arm straight and bend the left arm.
  5. Look towards your right hand and hold for a few seconds.
  6. Come back to the centre, then repeat on the left side.
Benefits
  • Improves spinal flexibility and mobility.
  • Relieves stiffness in the lower back.
  • Strengthens lumbar region and shoulders.
Precautions
  • Avoid with chronic spinal pain or injury.
  • Do not twist forcefully — move slowly.
  • Keep the knees slightly relaxed.
?Quick Viva
What does Katichakrasana mainly work on?
The waist (kati) and the lower back. It improves spinal flexibility.
Why should the movement be slow?
Because the spine is delicate. Slow movement prevents jerks and injuries.

More Asanas · Part I Pavanmuktasana & Bhujangasana

01

Pavanmuktasana

Wind-Relieving Pose

Good for: Obesity, Diabetes

How: Lie on your back. Bring both knees to your chest and hold them with your hands. Lift your head and touch your knees with your chin. Hold 5–10 seconds, then release slowly.

Benefits: Relieves gas and constipation, improves digestion, strengthens abdominal muscles.

Care: Avoid after abdominal surgery, with hernia or during pregnancy.

02

Bhujangasana

Cobra Pose

Good for: Obesity, Diabetes, Asthma, Hypertension

How: Lie on your stomach. Place palms beside your chest. Breathe in and lift your head and chest, keeping your navel on the floor. Hold 10–20 seconds, then lower slowly.

Benefits: Strengthens the spine and arms, opens the chest, improves digestion.

Care: Avoid with hernia, peptic ulcer, recent abdominal surgery or severe back injury.

?Quick Viva
Why is Pavanmuktasana called the "wind-relieving" pose?
Because it helps release trapped gas from the digestive system.
Why is Bhujangasana good for the spine?
It bends the spine backward and strengthens the muscles that support it.

Section 01 · Yoga

More Asanas · Part II Paschimottanasana & Gomukhasana

03

Paschimottanasana

Seated Forward Bend

Good for: Obesity, Diabetes

How: Sit with legs straight. Breathe in and raise your arms. Breathe out and bend forward from the hips, reaching towards your toes. Hold 10–20 seconds, then sit up slowly.

Benefits: Stretches the spine and hamstrings, improves digestion, helpful in diabetes management.

Care: Avoid with severe back pain, sciatica or recent abdominal surgery. Do not bounce while bending.

04

Gomukhasana

Cow Face Pose

Good for: Diabetes, Hypertension, Back Pain

How: Sit with legs crossed. Bring the right arm over the right shoulder and the left arm behind the back, clasping fingers if possible. Hold 10–15 seconds, then change sides.

Benefits: Stretches shoulders, chest and back; improves posture; relieves mild back stiffness.

Care: Avoid with severe shoulder, neck or back injury. Do not force the clasp.

?Quick Viva
What is Paschimottanasana mainly used for?
To stretch the back and hamstrings, and to help with digestion and diabetes.
Which body parts does Gomukhasana stretch?
Shoulders, chest, upper back and hips.

Section 01 · Yoga

General Benefits and Precautions Safe practice for everyone

General benefits of yoga

Physical
  • Improves flexibility and joint mobility.
  • Builds strength and endurance.
  • Improves balance and posture.
  • Helps digestion and metabolism.
Mental
  • Reduces stress and anxiety.
  • Improves concentration and memory.
  • Helps emotional balance.
  • Improves quality of sleep.

General precautions

  • Practise on an empty stomach, or at least 2–3 hours after a meal.
  • Wear loose, comfortable clothes.
  • Move slowly and stay within your comfort zone.
  • Avoid jerky movements and forceful stretching.
  • Consult a doctor first if you have any medical condition.
  • Practise on a non-slip surface.
  • Warm up before and relax after a session.
Important
Pregnant women, people who have had recent surgery, and people with chronic illness should practise yoga only under a trained teacher's supervision.

Section 02 · Khelo India

SAI Khelo India Fitness Test What it is and why it matters

What is it?

The SAI Khelo India Fitness Test is a nationwide fitness assessment for school students, run by the Sports Authority of India (SAI). It checks the fitness level of each student using standard, age-appropriate tests.

Why is it done?

  • To find out a student's fitness strengths and weaknesses.
  • To encourage daily physical activity.
  • To give schools and parents clear fitness data.
  • To spot students with talent for sports.

Two age-group batteries

The Khelo India test uses different tests for different age groups. This separation is required by CBSE:

Age groupClassesTests included
5–8 yearsI–IIIBMI, Flamingo Balance, Plate Tapping
9–18 yearsIV–XIIBMI, 50 m Dash, 600 m Run/Walk, Sit & Reach, Partial Curl-Up, Push-Ups (Boys) / Modified Push-Ups (Girls)
Note
Flamingo Balance and Plate Tapping are only for Classes I–III. They are not part of the Class IV–XII battery. For Class XII, focus on the six tests in the 9–18 group.
?Quick Viva
Who conducts the Khelo India Fitness Test?
The Sports Authority of India (SAI), under the Khelo India mission.
How many tests are in the Class IV–XII battery?
Six — BMI, 50 m Dash, 600 m Run/Walk, Sit & Reach, Partial Curl-Up, Push-Ups.
Why is fitness testing important?
It helps students know their fitness level and motivates them to improve.

Section 02 · Khelo India

Fitness Tests · Part 1 BMI · 50 m Dash · 600 m Run/Walk

1. Body Mass Index (BMI)

Measures: Weight relative to height. BMI is a screening indicator — it does not directly measure body fat.

Equipment: Weighing machine, stadiometer or measuring tape.

Procedure: Measure height (no shoes) to nearest 0.1 cm and weight to nearest 0.1 kg. Calculate:

BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ [Height (m)]2

Scoring: Record the BMI value; compare it with age- and gender-specific reference charts.

2. 50 m Dash

Measures: Acceleration and speed.

Equipment: Stopwatch, cones, a marked 50 m track.

Procedure: On the start signal, sprint 50 m as fast as possible. Time from the signal until the chest crosses the finish line.

Scoring: Time in seconds (nearest 0.1 s).

3. 600 m Run/Walk

Measures: Cardiovascular endurance.

Equipment: Stopwatch, cones, marked 200 m or 400 m track.

Procedure: Run or walk 600 m as fast as possible. Walking is allowed, but the goal is the shortest possible time.

Scoring: Time in minutes and seconds.

?Quick Viva
Does BMI measure body fat?
No. BMI is only an indicator based on height and weight. It does not tell you the exact body fat percentage.
Why is the 50 m dash used?
It is a short, quick test of speed and acceleration.

Fitness Tests · Part 2 Sit & Reach · Partial Curl-Up

4. Sit and Reach Test

Measures: Flexibility of the lower back and hamstring muscles.

Equipment: Sit-and-reach box (scale with 23 cm mark at the foot line), mat.

Procedure: Sit on the floor with legs straight and the soles of the feet against the box. Place one hand over the other, palms down. Reach forward slowly as far as possible. Hold 1–2 seconds.

Scoring: Distance reached in cm and mm.

Fig. 3 — Sit and Reach Test

5. Partial Curl-Up (30 seconds)

Measures: Abdominal muscular strength and endurance.

Equipment: Mat, stopwatch, marking tape.

Procedure: Lie on the back, knees bent at 90°, hands at the sides. Curl up at least 15 cm, then lower until the shoulder blades touch the floor. Do as many correct curl-ups as possible in 30 seconds.

Scoring: Correct repetitions in 30 seconds.

?Quick Viva
What does the sit-and-reach test measure?
Flexibility of the lower back and hamstrings.
Why is a partial curl-up used instead of a full sit-up?
The partial curl-up is safer for the lower back and still tests core strength well.

6. Push-Ups (Boys) / Modified Push-Ups (Girls)

Importance of Fitness Testing Why we measure fitness

Why fitness testing is useful

  • Self-awareness: You know where you stand physically.
  • Motivation: Real numbers encourage you to improve.
  • Health monitoring: It catches early signs of obesity or low flexibility.
  • Personalised training: Teachers can design activities based on needs.
  • Talent spotting: Students with strong scores in specific components get noticed.
  • Records: It tracks your fitness progress over years.

What we can learn from our own score

  • A low score is not a failure — it just shows which area needs work.
  • A high score in one test does not mean all-round fitness.
  • Fitness can always be improved with regular practice.
Remember
Fitness testing is a tool to help you improve, not a judgement about you.

To track progress and find out if fitness is improving or falling.

No. Each student should be compared with their own past performance and age-appropriate norms.

Introduction to Badminton A fast, skilful racket sport

What is badminton?

Badminton is a fast racket sport played by two players (singles) or two pairs (doubles). Players hit a shuttlecock over a net. A point is scored when the shuttle lands in the opponent's court, or when the opponent makes a fault.

Why is it popular?

  • It is one of the fastest racket sports in the world.
  • It builds speed, agility, endurance and coordination.
  • It can be played at any age — casually or competitively.
  • It has been an Olympic sport since 1992.
  • India has produced world-class players, which has boosted its popularity.
Fig. 4 — Racket and shuttlecock

How many players are there in a doubles match?
Two players on each side — four players in total.

Traditionally 16 goose feathers fixed in a cork base. Synthetic shuttles are also common now.

Section 03 · Badminton

History and Equipment How the game evolved

Brief history

Badminton grew from an old European game called battledore and shuttlecock. British officers in India in the 19th century played a version called Poona. The game was taken to England in the 1870s and was named after Badminton House in Gloucestershire. The Badminton World Federation (BWF) was formed in 1934, and badminton became an Olympic sport in 1992.

Main equipment

  • Racket: Light (usually 70–100 g), made of carbon fibre or light metal. It has a handle, shaft and stringed head.
  • Shuttlecock: 16 feathers fixed in a cork base. Synthetic shuttles are cheaper and more durable.
  • Net: 1.55 m high at the posts, 1.524 m at the centre.
  • Court: Rectangular surface with lines marked for singles and doubles.
Fig. 5 — Badminton net
?Quick Viva
When did badminton become an Olympic sport?
In 1992, at the Barcelona Olympics.
What is the net height at the centre?
1.524 m at the centre, 1.55 m at the posts.

Section 03 · Badminton

Rules and Regulations BWF Laws of Badminton

Scoring

  • A match is best of three games.
  • Each game is played to 21 points (rally-point scoring).
  • Every rally gives a point, whoever served.
  • At 20–20, a side must win by two clear points.
  • At 29–29, the first side to reach 30 points wins the game.

Service rules (BWF Law 9)

  • Server and receiver stand in diagonally opposite service courts, without touching the boundary lines.
  • Service height (Law 9.1.6): At the moment the shuttle is hit, the whole shuttle must be below 1.15 m from the court surface. This is a fixed height, not a "waist" height.
  • Racket direction (Law 9.1.7): At impact, the shaft of the racket must point in a downward direction.
  • The racket must first hit the base of the shuttle.
  • Both feet of the server and receiver must remain in contact with the floor and stationary until the service is delivered.

Common faults

  • Shuttle lands outside the court.
  • Shuttle fails to pass over the net.
  • Player touches the net with the racket or body.
  • Double hit or slinging action.
  • Feet of the server or receiver not in the correct position.
  • Serve hit above 1.15 m, or the racket shaft not pointing downward.
?Quick Viva
What is the service height limit?
The whole shuttle must be below 1.15 metres when it is hit.
At what score does a game end?
At 21 points, or if tied at 20–20, a side must lead by two points. At 29–29, the first to 30 wins.

Section 03 · Badminton

Basic Skills and Techniques How to hold and hit

Grips

  • Forehand grip: Hold the racket as if shaking hands — a natural, relaxed grip.
  • Backhand grip: Slightly rotate the hand so the thumb rests on the wider bevel of the handle.

Basic strokes

  • Clear: A high, deep shot to the opponent's backcourt.
  • Drop shot: A soft shot that just clears the net and falls into the frontcourt.
  • Smash: A powerful overhead shot hit steeply downward.
  • Drive: A fast, flat shot parallel to the floor.
  • Net shot: A gentle shot played from near the net.

Footwork

Good footwork lets you reach the shuttle early. Players use a small split-step, a chassé (side step) and a lunge to move smoothly around the court.

Section 03 · Badminton

Serving and Playing Techniques Starting the rally correctly

Types of serves

  • High serve: A deep, high serve to push the opponent to the backcourt.
  • Low serve: A soft serve that just clears the net and lands near the short service line.
  • Flick serve: Looks like a low serve but is flicked deep — used to surprise the opponent.
  • Drive serve: A fast, flat serve aimed at the opponent's body or backhand.

How to perform a high serve

  1. Stand sideways with the non-racket shoulder facing the net.
  2. Hold the shuttle in front of the body — the whole shuttle must be below 1.15 m.
  3. Draw the racket back and swing smoothly forward. The racket shaft must point downward at impact.
  4. Hit the shuttle gently upward so it travels high and deep.
  5. Follow through toward the target area.
Critical Rule
At impact, the whole shuttle must be below 1.15 m, and the racket shaft must point downward. The 1.15 m limit is a fixed height — not a body-relative "waist" height.

Playing positions

In singles, the player returns to the centre (base) after each shot. In doubles, players use a side-by-side formation when defending and a front-and-back formation when attacking.

?Quick Viva
What is a flick serve?
A deceptive serve that looks like a low serve but is hit deep to surprise the opponent.
What is the safe service height?
Below 1.15 metres — measured from the court surface to the whole shuttle.

Conclusion What I learnt from this project

This practical file covered three different but connected areas of physical education — yoga, fitness testing and a game. Each one taught me something useful for everyday life.

Yoga

Yoga showed me that good health is more than just the absence of illness. Simple asanas like Tadasana and Katichakrasana — done slowly and correctly — can help prevent and manage lifestyle diseases such as obesity, diabetes, asthma and back pain.

Final thought

All three topics reminded me that physical education is not just about performance. It is about building healthy habits — daily movement, correct breathing, good posture and regular fitness checks — that stay with us for life.
